Understanding Social Science - NCERT Notes

NCERT Pg. 1-2

In Grades 6 to 8, we explored Social Science through stories of people, places, and events. As you enter Grade 9, it is time to pause and understand what 'Social Science' truly means and why it matters.

Key NCERT Definition:

Human beings live in societies and depend on each other in many ways. Our lives are shaped by the environment, institutions, economic activities, and traditions. Social Science is the systematic study of human society, explaining why events occur, how governments function, and how past and present shape the world.

Social Science in Everyday Life

Every day we use houses, food, roads, schools, and electricity sourced and maintained through complex networks of farmers, factory workers, public authorities, and policies.

Natural Sciences

Physics, Chemistry, Biology (Study natural world)

Social Sciences

Society, institutions, cultures & human interaction

Understanding Society through Time & Traditions

NCERT Pg. 4-5

Over thousands of years, society transformed from early human hunting communities depending directly on nature to villages, towns, and cities with complex governance systems.

India's Knowledge Traditions & Pañchamahābhūtas

Early Indian thinkers valued questioning and logical reasoning. The concept of Pañchamahābhūtas describes the natural world as an interconnected system composed of five elements:

Prithvi
Earth
Apah
Water
Agni
Fire
Vāyu
Air
Akasha
Space

Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam & Arthashastra

"Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam" means 'the world is one family', expressing global interconnectedness. Similarly, Kautilya's Arthashastra (~2,300 years ago) examined governance, taxation, and state administration.

Social Science as a Study of Disciplines

NCERT Pg. 5-6

Human society is complex and cannot be explained by a single field. For example, a drought affects crops (environment), farmer incomes (economy), government relief (politics), and migration (society).

1. Geography

Studies Earth, environments, and human-surrounding relationships.

2. History

Examines the human past and societal changes over time.

3. Political Science

Analyses systems of governance, power, and citizen rights.

4. Economics

Explores production, distribution, and resource utilisation.

Geography: Space & Time

NCERT Pg. 6-7

Geography studies the location and distribution of places, objects, materials, and people, and human-environment relationships.

Key Approaches & Tools:

• Holistic & Spatial Perspective: Views world as interdependent systems.
• Tools: Atlases, maps, globes, GIS, infographics, and instruments.

What You Will Study in Grades 9-10:

Earth-shaping processes, landforms, atmosphere, climate, oceans, major biomes, India's biosphere reserves, and geospatial technologies.

History: Exploring the Human Past

NCERT Pg. 7-8

History is the study of the human past, traditions, and societal changes over time.

Itihāsa-Purāņa Tradition Preserves cultural memory and values through stories, offering identity and purpose.
Modern Historiography Relies on empirical evidence, human genetics, carbon-14 dating, and archaeology.

Sources of History:

Literary sources (memoirs, records, travelogues), Archaeological remains (monuments, artifacts, paintings).

Academex • Pratap Sanjay Sir Discipline Overview

Epigraphic & Numismatic Sources

NCERT Pg. 8

Epigraphic Sources (Inscriptions):

Texts, decrees, or records inscribed on durable materials like stone, metal plates, or rocks (e.g., Ashokan pillars, Gupta inscriptions).

Numismatic Sources (Coins & Currency):

Helps historians study economy, chronology, rulers, trade boundaries, and metal content (e.g., Samudragupta coins, Jahangir zodiac coins).

Political Science: Governance & Power

NCERT Pg. 9

Political Science is the study of governance, constitutions, power distribution, state institutions, and citizen rights.

Indian Political Thought:

Linked political authority with Dharma (moral duty), Artha (economic well-being), and Rajadharma (ruler duties). Articulated in Vedas, Mahabharata, Shukraniti, and Kautilya's Arthashastra.

Grassroots Democracy:

The Panchayati Raj System embodies grassroots democracy, giving citizens a direct voice in local self-governance.

Why Should We Study Social Science?

NCERT Pg. 11
  • Helps us understand systems created and managed by society (housing, water, roads, markets, digital spaces).
  • Explains variations in languages, customs, and occupations across regions while fostering national unity.
  • Equips citizens to understand laws, rights, and responsibilities in a democracy.
  • Strengthens ability to address shared challenges like environmental protection, public health, and urban growth.
